# What I've Tried

Before I go and sound like some mogul that's been through every language, let me preface by saying that I'm no 10 year dev. I've explored different languages like common folk explore Duolingo learning different languages. 

Now being through a non AI dominated era, and now experiencing language exploration in the current landscape - I have to say it's much more easy now to casually learn and explore. 

Pre AI domination, I started learning Rust as my first language. People told me that I was crazy, Dev influencers condemned it as a first language, and my coworkers insisted that I learn Python first. But, you know what, I just thought it was cool - that to me is enough. Fueled by motivation, I dove into official documentation like [rust-lang.org](rust-lang.org), lost myself in rust sub-reddits, and loved seeing hot malware and programs surfacing in the language I'd come to love the most.

Later I made an os switch to NixOS, and ***boy*** would I go back and not do that again. No hate on the the OS or even the language (nix), but for someone with no base in programming - that compiler made me lose some hair. I just was constantly unclear on what I did wrong and rust was so dang clear on that part even if it was strict that going here after rust was mind-numbing. I'm grateful for the experience and may eventually return to NixOS cause it's just such a great idea for keeping a clean, non drifting OS (I **am** back to Arch btw, in case you were curious lol).

As I started getting more serious about security, I eventually jumped two feet into [pwn.college](pwn.college). That's where I realized my bash skills were better than I was giving myself credit for and also where I learned assembly. Unfortunately, this is also where the road officially ended for Rust. 

As I lost myself to my coworkers' convictions and moved over to Python. This was also around the time that ChatGPT started taking off for programming and the vibe-coding era began. I also let Chat talk me into why Python would be better to learn for my goals and direction. I stuck with that and rode it out for about one and a half years. 

Now agentic coding is in rhythm and I've since had countless conversations evaluating which language will be the ***"perfect"*** landing point. Go is trending for speedy scanners, Java keeps its place as a historic monument, and C/C++ needs nothing to speak for it. 

# Reflections

I'm thankful that I took a course that taught me assembly, above anything else. Coding concepts like for loops, variables, and stuff is all cool and good - just like learning about the different cloud types and moon types there are when many of us were in middle school, but watching the rain and getting a telescope on the moon tell you truths beyond just an idea. I think learning assembly taught me the value that every programming language is the same at the end of the day - it's CPU and RAM operations (and more) and one CPU architecture is handled differently from another in ways that would require you to look beyond the surface. 

No not all programming languages are actually the same, but by the time they compile in the kernel, it's assembly -> or machine code of zeros and ones, if you want to be exact. 

# Where I Am Going Now, and What I'd do differently

I'm a bit of a health nut, philosophical, and holistic type of person. I find value in the spiritual journey and believe that as humans we owe ourselves the respect of giving our emotions attention as they are what drive us - not the input that comes from a keyboard, the voltage from a power chord, or the instructions baked into underlying services. With that said, I think there's importance in doing your own discovery and identifying a language that you find personal interest - not just the most utility language that you ***"should"*** learn. I found myself making the most progress and feeling the best when I was learning something I just genuinely liked. 

Although I find it necessary to become an expert in JavaScript for my bug bounty endeavors, my heart still lies in Rust. Beyond the cautionary tales that rust is too boilerplate for what I'm doing, I will proceed to learn it. The community is fun, funny, hard-working, and pretty positive with good outlooks on helping others. I've found refuge in that and I hope you find that in the language you seek. 

Although JavaScript is my highest priority, I will reach a point that my proficiency exceeds the time I need to spend learning it and studying and rather learning in the wild. At that time, I'll return to homeland, and invest in spinning up back ends that just seem way too cool - likely tying them into js front ends.
